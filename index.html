Chapter 1 · First Principles (Start Here)

Why OAuth Exists, The 4 Characters & 1 Golden Rule

New to OAuth? Perfect—no acronyms required yet. Every single authorization flow in modern software exists to solve one everyday problem without handing out your password.

1.1 Why Does OAuth Exist? (The Valet Key Story)

❌ Before OAuth (The Password Anti-Pattern)

Giving Away Your Master Key

In 2006, if a photo-printing website wanted to print pictures from your Google Photos, it literally asked you to type your Google email and password directly into the printing site. That third-party app could read your emails, delete your drive, or get hacked and leak your master password forever.

✅ With OAuth (The Temporary Valet Key)

Granting Narrow, Revocable Permission

Modern cars come with a valet key: it starts the engine and parks the car, without opening the glovebox or trunk. OAuth gives apps a digital valet key called an Access Token—letting the printing site read only your photos, for a short time, without ever seeing your password.

1.2 Meet the 4 Characters in Every OAuth Story

👤
RESOURCE OWNER (YOU)

1. The Data Owner

The person whose data is being protected and who decides whether to click “Allow”. (In automated background jobs, there is no human owner.)

e.g. Alice letting a calendar app view her meetings; a customer approving a bank payment.
💻
CLIENT APP · PUBLIC vs. CONFIDENTIAL

2. The App Asking for Access

The application requesting a valet key. Confidential apps run on secure backend servers that can safely lock away secrets; Public apps run in browsers or phones where anyone can inspect the app’s code.

e.g. Single-Page Web App (SPA) or mobile app (Public) vs. Backend Web Server or Scheduled Job (Confidential).
🏛️
AUTHORIZATION SERVER (THE GUARD)

3. The Security Desk

The trusted login service where you prove who you are, review what permissions the app wants, and which prints the temporary valet key (token).

e.g. “Sign in with Google”, Microsoft Entra ID, Okta, Auth0, Keycloak.
🗄️
RESOURCE SERVER (THE VAULT)

4. The API Hosting Your Data

The server storing your photos, orders, or bank balance. When an app knocks carrying a valet key, this server checks that it is real, unexpired, and meant for this door. Verifies signature, iss, aud, exp & scope.

e.g. Google Photos API, Microsoft Graph, Stripe API, SAP S/4HANA OData.

1.3 The Two Roads Messages Travel On

👁 Front-Channel · The Public Street (Browser Redirects)

Messages bounced through your web browser’s address bar when jumping from an app to the login page and back. Because browser extensions, history, and other apps on your phone can peek at URLs, OAuth never puts final secrets or long-lived tokens on this road.

🔒 Back-Channel · The Sealed Tunnel (Server-to-Server)

Encrypted calls sent directly from one backend server to another behind closed doors. Neither the user’s browser nor malicious extensions can see this traffic—so real tokens and private proofs are always exchanged here. (Uses POST /token over TLS, optionally paired with private_key_jwt or mTLS.)

1.4 One Golden Rule: Identity (Passport) vs. Permission (Keycard)

Knowing who someone is and knowing what they may access are two different questions—and OAuth splits them into two separate tokens.

AUTHORIZATION · PERMISSION

🏨 OAuth 2.0 / 2.1 — Hotel Keycard

  • Answers“What is this app allowed to do on the user’s behalf?”
  • Token IssuedAccess Token (like a keycard that opens Room 402 until noon).
  • Meant ForThe API (Resource Server)—the app just passes it along.
  • Who Reads ItOnly the API. Client apps should treat Access Tokens as opaque strings.
  • Asked For ByPermission scopes (e.g., orders:read, photos:view).
VS
AUTHENTICATION · IDENTITY

🛂 OpenID Connect (OIDC) — Passport

  • Answers“Who is this person, and when did they prove their identity?”
  • Token IssuedID Token (a signed identity card carrying name, email, login time).
  • Meant ForYour App itself so it can welcome Alice and start her session.
  • Who Reads ItYour App verifies signature, issuer, audience & .
  • Asked For ByAdding openid to the requested scope during login.
🚨 Golden Rule Warning: Never Use a Hotel Keycard as a Passport

Never log a user into your app simply because someone hands you a valid Access Token! An Access Token is addressed to an API, not to your app—so an attacker who steals an Access Token from a different leaky app could hand it to yours to impersonate Alice (Access Token Substitution). Always verify an ID Token (addressed to your app’s client_id) to sign a user in, and send the Access Token only to APIs.

1.5 What Is a “Grant Type”? (Your 3-Level Climb Ahead)

A Grant Type is simply the specific recipe an app uses to prove it deserves a token—because proving permission looks completely different for a user clicking “Allow” in a browser, a background server job, a corporate microservice, and a Smart TV.
